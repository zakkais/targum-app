Sources

The seforim and works this app is built on. Public-domain works link to free online texts; works still in print link to where they can be bought. Some works have no verified free edition or purchase page online, so they are listed without a link.

A Dictionary of the Targumim, the Talmud Babli and Yerushalmi, and the Midrashic Literature

Marcus Jastrow, 1903

Used for: Aramaic dictionary data. The classic English dictionary of Rabbinic Hebrew and Aramaic.

Volume 1 (Alef–Kaf) — Internet Archive Volume 2 (Lamed–Tav) — Internet Archive

Otzar Rashei Tevot אוצר ראשי תיבות

Shmuel Ashkenazi and Dov Yarden (Reuben Mass, Jerusalem)

Used for: abbreviations checklist. A thesaurus of Hebrew abbreviations, used to check that the app's abbreviation entries are complete.

Notrikon, vol. 1 נוטריקון

Heilprin / Stern (Alef–Tet)

Used for: Roshei Teivos data. A large dictionary of abbreviations; volume 1 covers Alef through Tet.

Notrikon, vol. 2 נוטריקון

Heilprin / Stern (Yud–Tav)

Used for: Roshei Teivos data. Volume 2 covers Yud through Tav.

Roshei Teivos

Stern

Used for: Roshei Teivos data. A dictionary of abbreviations, strong on Gemara abbreviations.

Lexicon Roshei Teivos

Baruch Halevi

Used for: Roshei Teivos data. A selective lexicon of abbreviations.

Luach Roshei Teivos Chabad לוח ראשי תיבות חב״ד

Friedman

Used for: Roshei Teivos data. Chabad-specific abbreviations.

Steinsaltz Abbreviations

R' Adin Steinsaltz

Used for: Roshei Teivos data. Abbreviations used in Kabbalah and Chassidus.

Sefer HaShorashim ספר השרשים

R' David Kimchi (Radak)

Used for: Hebrew grammar and roots (Dikduk section). Part of the Radak's מכלול (Mikhlol), arranged by root, with verb forms divided by binyan.

Berlin 1847 edition — Internet Archive

Sefer HaDikduk ספר הדקדוק

R' Moshe Chaim Luzzatto (Ramchal), 1740

Used for: Hebrew grammar (Dikduk section). Teaches grammar through the letters themselves: the roles each letter plays as a root letter, as a prefix, or as a suffix.

Ibn Ezra's Grammar Seforim

R' Avraham Ibn Ezra (12th century)

Used for: Hebrew grammar (Dikduk section). Sefer Moznayim ספר מאזנים (1140), Tzachot צחות (1145), and Sefer Safah Berurah ספר שפה ברורה — the first Hebrew grammar works written in Hebrew.

Sefer Safah Berurah — read online (Ben-Yehuda Project)

HaMaslul LeDikduk המסלול לדקדוק

Walder Education, Pavilion of Torah Umesorah, 2009

Used for: the binyan rules, conjugation tables, and examples in the Dikduk section. Seven workbooks, one per binyan, written for schoolchildren — the rules are short and plain, and the tables cover complete roots with the guttural and special cases. The rules here are paraphrased in our own words; the tables and quoted sources are transcribed from the workbooks.

Biur Shemos HaNirdafim ביאור שמות הנרדפים

R' Shlomo Aharon Wertheimer (Jerusalem, 1924)

Used for: the Thesaurus. Explains the synonyms of Tanach — when each word is used, in what era and context — not just lists of near-synonyms. The full first part (148 groups, 423 words) is in the app.

Grammar for Gemara and Targum Onkelos

Yitzhak Frank (3rd edition, Feldheim)

Used for: Aramaic grammar (Dikduk section). An introduction to Aramaic, covering the verb system, pronouns, noun states, and common Gemara forms.
