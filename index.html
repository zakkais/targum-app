שלום עליכם
HEBREW · PHRASE — ALSO USED IN YIDDISH
Peace be upon you. A greeting, especially on meeting after time apart. Reply: aleichem shalom.
Break-up: shalom (peace) + aleichem (upon you)
Root: Sh-L-M · Yiddish form: sholem-aleykhem
Example: Shalom aleichem — I have not seen you since last year.

Literal: learning; completion; what was learned and transmitted.
As a sefer: the Talmudic discussion and commentary on the Mishnah.
Root: G-M-R, to complete, to learn

סנהדרין
LOANWORD · FROM GREEK
The high court of 71 judges. From Greek synedrion, a council sitting together.
Used in: Mishnah, Gemara, Rambam — wherever the court is discussed
פרדס
LOANWORD · FROM PERSIAN
Orchard; walled garden. From Persian pairidaeza. Also the four levels of Torah study: Pshat, Remez, Drush, Sod.
Used in: Tanach, Gemara, Kabbalah
אפיקורוס
LOANWORD · FROM GREEK
A heretic; one who denies Torah fundamentals. From Greek Epicurus.
Used in: Mishnah, Gemara, Rambam

שמחה
HEBREW · NOUN
Joy; happiness; gladness. The general word — the ones below are its shades.
Root: Sh-M-Ch

SYNONYMS — WHEN TO USE WHICH

גילה
Gladness; exultation
Used when: rejoicing over good news, mostly in poetry and prophecy — Tehillim, Yeshayahu.
ששון
Joy; exultant gladness
Used when: the joy of celebration and deliverance; the classic pair is sasson v'simcha — Esther, Yirmiyahu.
חדוה
Delight; heartfelt joy
Used when: inner, settled joy — most famously the joy of Hashem, chedvas Hashem (Nechemiah).
עליצות
Merriment; exuberant rejoicing
Used when: outward, loud rejoicing; the rarest of the four, poetic.

BINYANIM AT A GLANCE
Paal — simple active — lamad, learned
Nifal — passive — nichtav, was written

Pual — passive of Piel — lumad, was taught
Hifil — causative — hichtiv, dictated
Hufal — passive of Hifil — huchtav, was dictated
Hitpael — reflexive — hitkateiv, corresponded
